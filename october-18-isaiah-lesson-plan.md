# October 18 Lesson Plan: The Redeemer Shall Come to Zion

**Reading:** Isaiah 58–66  
**Lesson emphasis:** Jesus Christ heals, delivers, gathers, and will return in glory. We can receive His power through sincere worship and help bring His light to others.

**Come, Follow Me lesson:** [October 12–18, 2026](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/come-follow-me-for-home-and-church-old-testament-2026/42?lang=eng)

## Lesson purpose

Give students room to share what they learned in Isaiah about Jesus Christ, fasting, the Sabbath, gathering Israel, and the hope of His return. Use the scripture slides as a quick, readable way to revisit a passage when needed.

## 25-Minute Lesson Plan

### 0:00–2:00 — Welcome and purpose

You might say:

> “In Isaiah 58–66, the Lord describes the kind of worship that changes us and blesses others. Most importantly, these chapters point us to Jesus Christ, who heals the brokenhearted, brings light, and will one day make all things new.”

Invite students to listen for what they learned about the Savior and how His power can shape their week.

### 2:00–5:00 — Quiet preparation

Invite students to review their scriptures or notes and write a brief response:

1. What verse, phrase, or image from Isaiah 58–66 stayed with me?
2. What does it teach me about Jesus Christ, worship, light, service, or hope?
3. What is one thought another person in the class should hear?

Ask students to write the scripture reference they plan to share.

### 5:00–18:00 — Class discussion

Invite students to share the insights they prepared. Keep the conversation centered on these questions:

- What did you notice about Jesus Christ?
- What does this passage teach about worship that changes us?
- How could this truth bless someone who feels burdened, discouraged, or far from God?

If the discussion needs a starting point, use one of these core passages:

- **Isaiah 61:1–3:** Jesus Christ heals, delivers, comforts, and gives beauty for ashes.
- **Isaiah 58:6–12:** True fasting includes relieving burdens and caring for those in need.
- **Isaiah 60:1–3, 19–20:** The Lord is our everlasting light, and His light gathers people to Him.

### 18:00–22:00 — Choose one additional passage

Use the scripture slides to read one passage together. Then ask one or two questions.

**Isaiah 58:13–14 — The Sabbath as a delight**

- What could make the Sabbath feel more like a delight in the Lord?
- What is the difference between merely avoiding our own pleasure and actively delighting in the Lord?

**Isaiah 59:16, 19–21 — The Redeemer comes to Zion**

- What do you learn from the Savior being called an “intercessor” and “Redeemer”?
- How does the Lord lift up a standard when darkness or opposition feels overwhelming?

**Isaiah 65:17–25 — A future of peace and joy**

- Which detail of Isaiah’s promised future gives you hope?
- What can we do now to prepare for the Savior’s return?

### 22:00–24:00 — Personal application

Invite students to silently choose one action for the coming week:

- Fast with a specific need or person in mind.
- Find one way to make the Sabbath more centered on the Lord.
- Turn to Jesus Christ with something that feels broken or heavy.
- Bring the Savior’s light to someone through an act of kindness or testimony.
- Prepare for the Second Coming by choosing greater faithfulness today.

Then ask:

> “What is one truth from Isaiah you want to carry into the coming week?”

### 24:00–25:00 — Conclusion

You might summarize:

> “Jesus Christ is the Redeemer Isaiah saw. He heals the brokenhearted, gives light in darkness, and will bring peace and joy when He comes again. As we worship Him sincerely and help others, we participate in His work.”

## Scripture passages (King James Version)

### Isaiah 58:6–12 — The fast the Lord has chosen

> **6** Is not this the fast that I have chosen? to loose the bands of wickedness, to undo the heavy burdens, and to let the oppressed go free, and that ye break every yoke?
>
> **7** Is it not to deal thy bread to the hungry, and that thou bring the poor that are cast out to thy house? when thou seest the naked, that thou cover him; and that thou hide not thyself from thine own flesh?
>
> **8** Then shall thy light break forth as the morning, and thine health shall spring forth speedily: and thy righteousness shall go before thee; the glory of the Lord shall be thy rearward.
>
> **9** Then shalt thou call, and the Lord shall answer; thou shalt cry, and he shall say, Here I am. If thou take away from the midst of thee the yoke, the putting forth of the finger, and speaking vanity;
>
> **10** And if thou draw out thy soul to the hungry, and satisfy the afflicted soul; then shall thy light rise in obscurity, and thy darkness be as the noonday:
>
> **11** And the Lord shall guide thee continually, and satisfy thy soul in drought, and make fat thy bones: and thou shalt be like a watered garden, and like a spring of water, whose waters fail not.
>
> **12** And they that shall be of thee shall build the old waste places: thou shalt raise up the foundations of many generations; and thou shalt be called, The repairer of the breach, The restorer of paths to dwell in.

**Discussion questions:**

- What stands out to you about the fast the Lord has chosen?
- What might it mean today to “undo the heavy burdens” or “let the oppressed go free”?
- How can fasting and caring for people in need work together?
- Which promise in verses 8–12 would be meaningful to someone who feels spiritually dry or discouraged?
- What could it look like for us to be “repairer[s] of the breach” this week?

### Isaiah 58:13–14 — The Sabbath as a delight

> **13** If thou turn away thy foot from the sabbath, from doing thy pleasure on my holy day; and call the sabbath a delight, the holy of the Lord, honourable; and shalt honour him, not doing thine own ways, nor finding thine own pleasure, nor speaking thine own words:
>
> **14** Then shalt thou delight thyself in the Lord; and I will cause thee to ride upon the high places of the earth, and feed thee with the heritage of Jacob thy father: for the mouth of the Lord hath spoken it.

**Discussion questions:**

- What does it mean to call the Sabbath “a delight”?
- How is delighting in the Lord different from simply making a list of things we should not do?
- What helps you feel closer to the Savior on the Sabbath?
- What is one small change that could make the Lord’s day more joyful or meaningful?

### Isaiah 59:16, 19–21 — The Redeemer comes to Zion

> **16** And he saw that there was no man, and wondered that there was no intercessor: therefore his arm brought salvation unto him; and his righteousness, it sustained him.
>
> **19** So shall they fear the name of the Lord from the west, and his glory from the rising of the sun. When the enemy shall come in like a flood, the Spirit of the Lord shall lift up a standard against him.
>
> **20** And the Redeemer shall come to Zion, and unto them that turn from transgression in Jacob, saith the Lord.
>
> **21** As for me, this is my covenant with them, saith the Lord; My spirit that is upon thee, and my words which I have put in thy mouth, shall not depart out of thy mouth, nor out of the mouth of thy seed, nor out of the mouth of thy seed’s seed, saith the Lord, from henceforth and for ever.

**Discussion questions:**

- What do the words “intercessor” and “Redeemer” teach you about Jesus Christ?
- What does it mean to turn from transgression and come to the Redeemer?
- When have you seen the Spirit of the Lord “lift up a standard” against fear, temptation, or discouragement?
- How does the Lord’s covenant in verse 21 strengthen families and future generations?

### Isaiah 60:1–3, 19–20 — The Lord is our everlasting light

> **1** Arise, shine; for thy light is come, and the glory of the Lord is risen upon thee.
>
> **2** For, behold, the darkness shall cover the earth, and gross darkness the people: but the Lord shall arise upon thee, and his glory shall be seen upon thee.
>
> **3** And the Gentiles shall come to thy light, and kings to the brightness of thy rising.
>
> **19** The sun shall be no more thy light by day; neither for brightness shall the moon give light unto thee: but the Lord shall be unto thee an everlasting light, and thy God thy glory.
>
> **20** Thy sun shall no more go down; neither shall thy moon withdraw itself: for the Lord shall be thine everlasting light, and the days of thy mourning shall be ended.

**Discussion questions:**

- What forms of darkness do people experience today?
- What does it mean that the Savior is an “everlasting light”?
- How can we “arise” and reflect the Lord’s light without drawing attention to ourselves?
- How can the gospel of Jesus Christ help gather someone from darkness to light?
- Which phrase in these verses gives you the most hope? Why?

### Isaiah 61:1–3 — The Savior’s mission

> **1** The Spirit of the Lord God is upon me; because the Lord hath anointed me to preach good tidings unto the meek; he hath sent me to bind up the brokenhearted, to proclaim liberty to the captives, and the opening of the prison to them that are bound;
>
> **2** To proclaim the acceptable year of the Lord, and the day of vengeance of our God; to comfort all that mourn;
>
> **3** To appoint unto them that mourn in Zion, to give unto them beauty for ashes, the oil of joy for mourning, the garment of praise for the spirit of heaviness; that they might be called trees of righteousness, the planting of the Lord, that he might be glorified.

**Discussion questions:**

- How did Jesus Christ fulfill this prophecy during His earthly ministry?
- Which part of the Savior’s mission in these verses means the most to you today?
- What might “beauty for ashes” look like in a person’s life?
- How does the Savior bind up a broken heart even when a difficult situation continues?
- How can we help someone feel the comfort and liberty Jesus Christ offers?

### Isaiah 65:17–25 — A future of peace and joy

> **17** For, behold, I create new heavens and a new earth: and the former shall not be remembered, nor come into mind.
>
> **18** But be ye glad and rejoice for ever in that which I create: for, behold, I create Jerusalem a rejoicing, and her people a joy.
>
> **19** And I will rejoice in Jerusalem, and joy in my people: and the voice of weeping shall be no more heard in her, nor the voice of crying.
>
> **20** There shall be no more thence an infant of days, nor an old man that hath not filled his days: for the child shall die an hundred years old; but the sinner being an hundred years old shall be accursed.
>
> **21** And they shall build houses, and inhabit them; and they shall plant vineyards, and eat the fruit of them.
>
> **22** They shall not build, and another inhabit; they shall not plant, and another eat: for as the days of a tree are the days of my people, and mine elect shall long enjoy the work of their hands.
>
> **23** They shall not labour in vain, nor bring forth for trouble; for they are the seed of the blessed of the Lord, and their offspring with them.
>
> **24** And it shall come to pass, that before they call, I will answer; and while they are yet speaking, I will hear.
>
> **25** The wolf and the lamb shall feed together, and the lion shall eat straw like the bullock: and dust shall be the serpent’s meat. They shall not hurt nor destroy in all my holy mountain, saith the Lord.

**Discussion questions:**

- What details of the Lord’s promised future give you hope?
- Why do you think Isaiah repeatedly uses words such as “glad,” “rejoice,” and “joy” in this description?
- What do you learn from the promise that God will answer before His people call?
- How does the Savior’s future reign change the way we face sorrow, injustice, or uncertainty now?
- What can we do today to prepare for the Savior’s return and the peace He will bring?

## Teaching reminders

- Let students respond to one another before responding yourself.
- Do not try to discuss every passage. One meaningful conversation is enough.
- Use the scripture slides only when they serve the class discussion.
- Keep returning to the central question: “What does this teach us about Jesus Christ and His power to heal, gather, and bring joy?”
