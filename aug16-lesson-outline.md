# August 10-16 Lesson Plan

## Yet Will I Trust in Him

**Scriptures:** Job 1-3; 12-14; 19; 21-24; 38-40; 42

**Central message:** The book of Job does not give a simple explanation for every kind of suffering. It teaches that faith can continue while questions remain unanswered. Job grieved honestly, rejected false explanations, testified of his living Redeemer, and learned that God's wisdom and perspective are greater than his own.

**Key phrase:**

> "Though he slay me, yet will I trust in him." - Job 13:15

## The Story at a Glance

Job is introduced as a righteous man who feared God and avoided evil. He lost his property, children, and health. His wife and friends struggled to make sense of his suffering. At first, his friends mourned with him in silence, but then they insisted that Job must have sinned and that his suffering was punishment.

Job rejected their explanation. He did not understand why he was suffering, and he spoke honestly about his pain, but he continued to reach toward God. In the middle of that pain, Job testified, "I know that my redeemer liveth" (Job 19:25). Later, the Lord spoke to Job and helped him see that God's wisdom, power, and perspective are far greater than human understanding.

## Important Background

Job is part of the Old Testament's wisdom literature. Like Proverbs and Ecclesiastes, it explores big questions about life, righteousness, suffering, justice, and God. Much of Job is written as poetry, not as a fast-moving historical narrative. That matters because the book is not trying to give a simple timeline or a quick answer. It invites readers to wrestle carefully with hard questions.

The setting is intentionally ancient and somewhat distant from Israel's later history. Job lives in "the land of Uz," and the book does not connect him to Moses, the law of Moses, the temple, Jerusalem, or the kings of Israel. Job functions almost like a universal story about a faithful person facing suffering that does not seem fair.

The opening chapters introduce Satan as an adversary and accuser. For teaching purposes, it may help to explain that the Come, Follow Me manual cautions us not to read this as a normal literal conversation between God and Satan. The point is not that God casually bargains over Job's suffering. The point is that Job's faith is being tested, and the reader is told from the beginning that Job's suffering is not because he is secretly wicked.

Job's friends represent a common but incomplete belief: righteous people prosper, wicked people suffer, and therefore suffering must prove that someone has sinned. There is truth in the idea that choices have consequences, but Job shows that this explanation is too small. Some suffering comes because of our choices, some because of others' choices, some because of mortality, and some for reasons we do not fully understand now.

The structure of the book helps the lesson flow:

- **Job 1-2:** Job is introduced as righteous, and his losses begin.
- **Job 3:** Job laments. He is honest about his anguish.
- **Job 4-31:** Job and his friends argue. The friends keep trying to explain his suffering by accusing him.
- **Job 32-37:** Elihu, another speaker, offers his perspective.
- **Job 38-41:** The Lord answers Job, not by explaining every event, but by revealing His greater wisdom and power.
- **Job 42:** Job responds with humility, prays for his friends, and is blessed by the Lord.

This background matters because students may hear simplified explanations for suffering. Job gives permission to be honest about pain while still refusing to give up faith in God. It also teaches us to be careful when someone else is hurting. We do not always know why a trial has come, but we can choose to comfort, strengthen, and point people to Jesus Christ rather than accuse or explain too quickly.

## Recommended 40-45 Minute Flow

- **5 minutes:** Introduce Job and the question of suffering
- **8 minutes:** Job's first responses: grief and worship
- **7 minutes:** Job's friends and Christlike comfort
- **8 minutes:** "Yet will I trust in him"
- **8 minutes:** "I know that my Redeemer lives"
- **7 minutes:** God's larger perspective
- **2 minutes:** Closing testimony and invitation

If time is short, focus on sections 1, 3, 4, and 5.

## Opening: Trust Without Full Understanding

Ask:

**How can you keep trusting God when you do not understand what is happening?**

Explain that Job is about one of the hardest questions people ask: Why do painful things happen to faithful people? The book does not answer every part of that question, but it shows what faith can look like while we wait for more understanding.

## 1. Job Was Faithful Before the Trial

**Read:** Job 1:1-3, 10

**Listen for:** How is Job described before anything bad happens? What blessings did he have?

### Teaching points

- The book intentionally tells us Job was upright before describing his suffering.
- Job had family, wealth, servants, reputation, and a blessed household.
- Job 1:10 describes the Lord blessing the work of Job's hands and increasing his substance.
- Job's pain was not presented as punishment for hidden wickedness.
- This helps correct the assumption that every hard thing is evidence of personal failure.

### Discuss

- Why is it important to know Job was righteous before the trials came?
- Why does it matter that Job had much to lose?
- Why can it be harmful to assume we know why someone is suffering?

### Optional video

**Watch:** [Jesus Christ Has Overcome Unfairness for Us All](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/video/easter-videos/he-is-risen-renlund?lang=eng), Elder Dale G. Renlund, 2:30

Use this after discussing why Job's suffering should not be reduced to a simple accusation. Ask students to listen for what Jesus Christ understands about unfairness and what He can make right.

**Transition:** Job's first response is not emotionless. He mourns deeply and still turns toward God.

## 2. Faith Does Not Cancel Grief

**Read:** Job 1:13-22; 2:7-10

**Listen for:** What did Job lose? What did Job do and say while grieving?

### Teaching points

- Job lost animals, servants, property, children, and health.
- The messengers came one after another, showing how overwhelming the losses were.
- Job rent his mantle and shaved his head, both signs of real mourning.
- Job worshipped even though he did not understand.
- Trusting God does not mean pretending everything is fine.
- Job's integrity was not based only on receiving blessings.

### Discuss

- What is the difference between trusting God and pretending everything is fine?
- How can faith and grief exist at the same time?

**Transition:** The next part of the story introduces Job's friends. They begin well, but later their words become part of Job's pain.

## 3. Good Friends Strengthen Instead of Accuse

**Read:** Job 2:11-13; 16:2, 5

**Listen for:** What did Job's friends do well at first? What changed later?

### Background

At first, Job's friends came to mourn and comfort him. They sat with him in silence for seven days because they saw that his grief was very great. That was compassionate. Later, they tried to explain his suffering by accusing him of wrongdoing. Job called them "miserable comforters."

### Teaching points

- Presence can be a powerful form of comfort.
- Not every situation needs an explanation from us.
- Words can either strengthen grief-stricken people or make their burden heavier.
- Christlike comfort starts with compassion, humility, and patience.

### Discuss

- What kind of words help someone who is hurting?
- What should we avoid saying when we do not understand someone else's trial?

**Transition:** Job does not have all the answers, but he holds tightly to what he knows about God.

## 4. "Yet Will I Trust in Him"

**Read:** Job 12:10, 13, 16; 13:15-16

**Listen for:** What did Job know about God?

### Teaching points

- Job believed life is in God's hands.
- Job trusted that God has wisdom, strength, counsel, and understanding.
- Job's statement "yet will I trust in him" is not casual optimism. It is faith spoken from pain.
- Trust in God can coexist with questions, confusion, and pleading.

### Discuss

- What truths about God help you trust Him when life is confusing?
- Why is trust sometimes a choice before it becomes a feeling?

**Transition:** One of Job's clearest testimonies is about Jesus Christ, his Redeemer.

## 5. Jesus Christ Is My Redeemer

**Read:** Job 14:14-15; 19:25-27

**Listen for:** What hope does Job express about life after death and seeing God?

### Background

The word "redeemer" can refer to someone who rescues, vindicates, or restores. For us, Job's testimony points clearly to Jesus Christ. Christ redeems us from sin and death, and because of His Resurrection, we will live again.

Job's testimony is striking because it comes while he is still suffering. He does not say, "I know my Redeemer lives" only after life becomes easy. He says it while he feels abandoned, misunderstood, and physically broken.

### Activity: Names of Christ

Give each student a sheet of paper. Invite them to spend about five minutes writing as many names and titles of Jesus Christ as they can think of.

Examples to get them started: **Christ, Redeemer, Lord, Savior.**

After they write, invite them to circle one name that feels especially meaningful to them right now. Ask:

**How does that name of Christ help you trust Him?**

Possible names students may think of include Redeemer, Savior, Lord, Messiah, Lamb of God, Prince of Peace, Good Shepherd, Creator, Advocate, Mediator, King of Kings, Light of the World, Bread of Life, Living Water, Son of God, Son of Man, Jehovah, Immanuel, Rock, Deliverer, and Healer.

### Teaching points

- Jesus Christ lives.
- Because He lives, death is not the end.
- The Resurrection gives hope even when mortal life is painful or unfair.
- Faith in Christ gives Job a future beyond his present suffering.

### Discuss

- What difference does it make to know that the Redeemer lives?
- How does the Resurrection change the way we see suffering and death?

**Transition:** Job still wanted answers. In chapters 38-40, the Lord answers in a way that changes Job's perspective.

## 6. God's Perspective Is Greater Than Mine

**Read:** Job 23:8-12; 38:1-7; 40:1-5; 42:1-6

**Listen for:** What did Job learn about God and about himself?

### Background

The Lord did not give Job a detailed explanation of every loss. Instead, He asked Job questions about creation, the earth, the sea, light, weather, stars, and living creatures. These questions helped Job see the limits of human understanding and the greatness of God's wisdom.

This does not mean Job's pain was unimportant. It means Job was invited to trust a God whose knowledge and power are far greater than his own.

### Teaching points

- Job could not always perceive God, but God knew the way Job took.
- God sees more than we see.
- God's answer enlarged Job's view rather than simplifying every problem.
- Humility before God can bring peace even before every question is answered.

### Discuss

- What helps a person keep walking in God's way during a hard season?
- Why might a larger view of God help when a trial feels overwhelming?

## 7. The Ending of Job

**Read:** Job 42:10-11

### Teaching points

- Job prayed for his friends even after they had hurt him with their words.
- His family and acquaintances came to comfort him.
- The Lord blessed Job, but the ending does not erase the reality of what Job suffered.
- The deeper message is not simply that every earthly loss is quickly replaced. The deeper message is that Job remained in relationship with God.

### Discuss

- What does Job praying for his friends teach us?
- Why should we be careful not to turn Job's ending into an easy answer for suffering?

## Closing Discussion

Return to the opening question:

**What helps us trust God when we do not understand everything?**

Draw out the connected answers:

- Remember what we know about God's wisdom and goodness
- Keep praying honestly
- Let grief and faith coexist
- Strengthen others instead of judging them
- Hold to Jesus Christ, our living Redeemer
- Trust that God's perspective is greater than ours

## Closing Testimony

Job teaches that questions can coexist with faith. Jesus Christ is our Redeemer. Because He lives, suffering, death, confusion, and unfairness do not have the final word. We may not always know why things happen, but we can know who God is, and we can choose to trust Him.

Invite each person to complete this sentence privately:

> **Because my Redeemer lives, I can trust Him with __________.**
