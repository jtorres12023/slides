# September 27 Lesson Plan: A Marvellous Work and a Wonder

**Reading:** Isaiah 13-14; 22; 24-30; 35  
**Lesson emphasis:** Jesus Christ restores, heals, and gives hope. We can participate in His marvellous work.

**Come, Follow Me lesson:** [September 21-27, 2026](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/come-follow-me-for-home-and-church-old-testament-2026/39?lang=eng)

## Lesson purpose

Give students room to share what they discovered in Isaiah about the Savior, the Restoration, and the Lord's power to restore what is lost or broken. Use the backup passages only if the discussion needs a starting point.

## 25-Minute Lesson Plan

### 0:00-2:00 - Welcome and purpose

You might say:

> “Isaiah includes warnings about pride and turning away from God, but it is also full of hope. Today we will learn from what we each discovered about Jesus Christ, healing, restoration, and the marvellous work the Lord is doing in our day.”

Students can share:

- A verse, phrase, or image that stood out
- Something they learned about Jesus Christ
- A question they are still considering
- A connection to their own life
- Something they discussed at home

### 2:00-5:00 - Quiet preparation

Invite students to review their scriptures or notes and write brief responses:

1. Which verse or image from Isaiah stayed with me?
2. What does it teach about the Savior, repentance, healing, the Restoration, or hope?
3. What is one thought another person in the class should hear?

Ask students to write the scripture reference they plan to share.

### 5:00-21:00 - Class discussion

Invite students to share the passage or insight they prepared. Keep the conversation centered on these simple questions:

- What did you notice about Jesus Christ?
- What does this passage teach about healing, hope, or the Restoration?
- How could this truth help someone this week?

If discussion slows, choose one backup passage below and ask one of its questions.

### If the class needs help getting started

Choose **one** passage below. Read it together, invite students to notice a word or phrase, and use one or two discussion questions. Do not try to cover every passage.

If you want a focused set of core passages, consider using Isaiah 29:13-14, 18-24; Isaiah 25:8-9 or Isaiah 26:19; Isaiah 30:18-21; and Isaiah 35:3-10.

### 21:00-24:00 - Personal application

Invite students to silently choose one action for the coming week:

- Turn to the Savior with something that feels broken, heavy, or discouraging.
- Thank the Lord for a blessing of the restored gospel.
- Read or share a Book of Mormon passage that strengthens faith in Jesus Christ.
- Look for a way to bring hope or peace to someone else.
- Choose a humble response when pride or self-reliance pulls you away from the Lord.

Then ask:

> “What is one truth from Isaiah that you want to carry into the coming week?”

### 24:00-25:00 - Conclusion

You might summarize:

> “Isaiah teaches that the Savior is our refuge, our sure foundation, and the One who can restore what is lost or broken. The Restoration of His gospel truly is a marvellous work and a wonder, and we are part of it.”

## Backup Passage Notes and Discussion Questions

### Isaiah 22:22-23 - The Messiah Holds the Key

> “And the key of the house of David will I lay upon his shoulder; so he shall open, and none shall shut; and he shall shut, and none shall open. And I will fasten him as a nail in a sure place; and he shall be for a glorious throne to his father's house.”

**Main idea:** Isaiah's imagery points to the Messiah. Jesus Christ has divine authority and is a sure, reliable foundation.

**Discussion questions:**

- What does a key allow someone to do? What might this teach about the Savior's authority?
- Why is “a sure place” a meaningful image for Jesus Christ?
- How can trusting the Savior's authority bring peace when life feels uncertain?
- What helps you build your life on Christ as a sure foundation?

### Isaiah 14:3 - Rest from Sorrow and Fear

> “And it shall come to pass in the day that the Lord shall give thee rest from thy sorrow, and from thy fear, and from the hard bondage wherein thou wast made to serve.”

**Main idea:** The Savior offers rest from sorrow, fear, and spiritual bondage.

**Discussion questions:**

- What kinds of sorrow, fear, or bondage might people experience today?
- What does the Lord's promise of “rest” mean to you?
- How can the Savior give rest even when a difficult situation has not ended?
- What helps you turn to Jesus Christ when you feel overwhelmed or afraid?

### Isaiah 25:8-9 - The Savior Swallows Up Death

> “He will swallow up death in victory; and the Lord God will wipe away tears from off all faces; and the rebuke of his people shall he take away from off all the earth: for the Lord hath spoken it. And it shall be said in that day, Lo, this is our God; we have waited for him, and he will save us: this is the Lord; we have waited for him, we will be glad and rejoice in his salvation.”

**Main idea:** Through His Atonement and Resurrection, Jesus Christ overcomes death and offers comfort and salvation.

**Discussion questions:**

- Which phrase in these verses gives you the most hope? Why?
- What does it mean that the Savior will “wipe away tears from off all faces”?
- How do the Atonement and Resurrection of Jesus Christ change the way we face grief or loss?
- What does it look like to “rejoice in his salvation” in everyday life?

### Isaiah 28:16 - Jesus Christ, a Sure Foundation

> “Therefore thus saith the Lord God, Behold, I lay in Zion for a foundation a stone, a tried stone, a precious corner stone, a sure foundation: he that believeth shall not make haste.”

**Main idea:** Jesus Christ is the reliable foundation on whom we can build our lives.

**Discussion questions:**

- What makes a foundation important in a building? How does that compare to our lives?
- What do the words “tried,” “precious,” and “sure” teach about Jesus Christ?
- What pressures or fears can make us feel that we need to “make haste”?
- How does faith in Christ help us make steady, wise choices?

### Isaiah 26:3-4, 19 - Peace, Trust, and Resurrection

> “Thou wilt keep him in perfect peace, whose mind is stayed on thee: because he trusteth in thee. Trust ye in the Lord for ever: for in the Lord Jehovah is everlasting strength. Thy dead men shall live, together with my dead body shall they arise. Awake and sing, ye that dwell in dust: for thy dew is as the dew of herbs, and the earth shall cast out the dead.”

**Main idea:** The Savior gives peace and lasting strength to those who trust Him, and He makes Resurrection possible for all.

**Discussion questions:**

- What might it mean to keep your mind “stayed” on the Lord?
- How is perfect peace different from a life without challenges?
- What does “everlasting strength” mean to you?
- How does the promise of Resurrection change the way we face death or grief?

### Isaiah 29:13-14, 18-24 - A Marvellous Work and a Wonder

> “Wherefore the Lord said, Forasmuch as this people draw near me with their mouth, and with their lips do honour me, but have removed their heart far from me, and their fear toward me is taught by the precept of men: Therefore, behold, I will proceed to do a marvellous work among this people, even a marvellous work and a wonder: for the wisdom of their wise men shall perish, and the understanding of their prudent men shall be hid. And in that day shall the deaf hear the words of the book, and the eyes of the blind shall see out of obscurity, and out of darkness. The meek also shall increase their joy in the Lord, and the poor among men shall rejoice in the Holy One of Israel. For the terrible one is brought to nought, and the scorner is consumed, and all that watch for iniquity are cut off: That make a man an offender for a word, and lay a snare for him that reproveth in the gate, and turn aside the just for a thing of nought. Therefore thus saith the Lord, who redeemed Abraham, concerning the house of Jacob, Jacob shall not now be ashamed, neither shall his face now wax pale. But when he seeth his children, the work of mine hands, in the midst of him, they shall sanctify my name, and sanctify the Holy One of Jacob, and shall fear the God of Israel. They also that erred in spirit shall come to understanding, and they that murmured shall learn doctrine.”

**Main idea:** The Lord restored His gospel in a marvellous work that invites us to draw near to Him with sincere hearts.

**Discussion questions:**

- What is the difference between honoring God with our lips and drawing near to Him with our hearts?
- What parts of the Restoration feel “marvellous” or wonderful to you?
- How has the restored gospel helped you know or follow Jesus Christ?
- What can we do to keep our hearts close to the Lord, not merely our outward behavior?
- What blessings of spiritual sight, understanding, joy, or learning do you see in verses 18-24?

### Isaiah 30:18-21 - The Lord Waits to Be Gracious and Guides Us

> “And therefore will the Lord wait, that he may be gracious unto you, and therefore will he be exalted, that he may have mercy upon you: for the Lord is a God of judgment: blessed are all they that wait for him. For the people shall dwell in Zion at Jerusalem: thou shalt weep no more: he will be very gracious unto thee at the voice of thy cry; when he shall hear it, he will answer thee. And though the Lord give you the bread of adversity, and the water of affliction, yet shall not thy teachers be removed into a corner any more, but thine eyes shall see thy teachers: And thine ears shall hear a word behind thee, saying, This is the way, walk ye in it, when ye turn to the right hand, and when ye turn to the left.”

**Main idea:** The Lord is patient, merciful, and eager to bless those who turn to Him and wait on Him.

**Discussion questions:**

- What do you learn about the Lord's character from this verse?
- What might it mean to wait for the Lord without becoming passive or discouraged?
- When is it hardest to trust the Lord's timing?
- How have you seen mercy or guidance after patiently turning to the Lord?
- What are some ways the Lord might help us hear, “This is the way, walk ye in it”?

### Isaiah 35:3-10 - Strength, Healing, and the Way of Holiness

> “Strengthen ye the weak hands, and confirm the feeble knees. Say to them that are of a fearful heart, Be strong, fear not: behold, your God will come with vengeance, even God with a recompence; he will come and save you. Then the eyes of the blind shall be opened, and the ears of the deaf shall be unstopped. Then shall the lame man leap as an hart, and the tongue of the dumb sing: for in the wilderness shall waters break out, and streams in the desert. And the parched ground shall become a pool, and the thirsty land springs of water: in the habitation of dragons, where each lay, shall be grass with reeds and rushes. And an highway shall be there, and a way, and it shall be called The way of holiness; the unclean shall not pass over it; but it shall be for those: the wayfaring men, though fools, shall not err therein. No lion shall be there, nor any ravenous beast shall go up thereon, it shall not be found there; but the redeemed shall walk there: And the ransomed of the Lord shall return, and come to Zion with songs and everlasting joy upon their heads: they shall obtain joy and gladness, and sorrow and sighing shall flee away.”

**Main idea:** Jesus Christ brings strength, healing, and life-giving hope to people who feel weak or afraid.

**Discussion questions:**

- What images of healing or renewal stand out to you in these verses?
- How did Jesus Christ fulfill these promises during His earthly ministry?
- How can the Savior heal people spiritually, emotionally, or physically?
- What could it look like to strengthen weak hands or encourage someone with a fearful heart this week?
- What do “The way of holiness” and the redeemed returning with joy teach about discipleship?

## Teaching reminders

- Let students respond to one another before responding yourself.
- Ask students to share what they learned, not what they think the teacher wants to hear.
- Do not feel pressure to cover every passage.
- Use one backup passage when needed instead of turning the lesson into a lecture.
- Keep returning to the central question: “What does this teach us about Jesus Christ and His power to restore?”
